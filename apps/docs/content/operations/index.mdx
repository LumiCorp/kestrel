Deploy the runtime, understand what it is doing, and keep the user path recoverable when something fails. Kestrel runs locally through Local Core and remotely through a runner service; these guides cover the controls and evidence required to operate both forms.

## Prepare and deploy

Start with [local and remote runner connections](/operate/runner-service), then configure [environment and authentication](/operate/environment-and-auth), [preflight migrations](/operate/migrations), and review the [production operating model](/operate/deployment). Keep runner and provider credentials on trusted servers, record exact release identity, and prove the request path before serving users.

## Investigate and recover

Locate the affected request with [Observability](/operate/observability), structure the incident with [Reliability](/operate/reliability), inspect persisted evidence with [Replay](/operate/replay), and use [Troubleshooting](/operate/troubleshooting) when you need to begin from the visible symptom.

## Authority and security

Use [Environment and auth](/operate/environment-and-auth), [Model authority](/operate/model-authority), and [Security](/operate/security) to assign profiles, providers, tools, approvals, execution boundaries, and network egress to their owning services.

## Control and quality

Use [Operator control](/operate/operator-control) to act on work already in flight. Compare behavior before and after a change with [Ruhroh evaluations](/operate/evaluations) and [quality gates](/operate/quality-gates). Kestrel One model operators should also understand [credential leases](/operate/credential-leases) and [model access decisions](/operate/model-authority).

Mission Control owns project work and acceptance; [Budgets and allocations](/operate/budgets-and-allocations) own spend authority and ledger evidence.

## Release and rollback

Use [Release management](/operate/release-management) to prepare an exact revision, promote verified Vercel and Fly artifacts, run canaries, retain evidence, and roll back or fix forward without rebuilding the candidate.

  <p>You can identify the owning operational boundary and the exact playbook for a deployment, incident, control action, budget, or release.</p>
